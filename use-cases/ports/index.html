Use case · Ports and port rail

Secure links for terminals, yards and port rail, ready for quantum.

A port runs on links that are hard to secure: yard and gate equipment on wireless and cellular, remote sites across the waterfront, and the rail interchange where short lines move boxes in and out. Those links drop. Much of the equipment around them was built elsewhere and runs for decades. The Coast Guard now expects a written cybersecurity assessment and plan for every regulated facility.

Why port security teams can take a look

  • Patent pending (U.S. Application No. 64/175,794) for keeping secure links up on degraded networks.
  • SOF RACER member. Eigen-Systems, Inc. is a US-owned company in Omaha, Nebraska, and a member of the U.S. Special Operations Command's SOF RACER industry network.
  • The deadlines are dated. The Coast Guard's Marine Transportation System cybersecurity rule took effect July 16, 2025. MTSA-regulated facilities must complete a Cybersecurity Assessment and submit a Cybersecurity Plan by July 16, 2027 [1]. A 2024 House investigation documented unauthorized cellular modems on some ship-to-shore cranes at U.S. ports [2].

Where EigenTunnel helps

  • Know your encryption. EigenTunnel Scanner runs fully air-gapped on site and lists weak and quantum-vulnerable encryption on a network segment you choose. The results stay with you and can feed your Cybersecurity Assessment.
  • Protect the links you own. EigenTunnel adds quantum-resistant encryption to a chosen connection, such as yard to terminal office, gate to operations, or port rail interchange to dispatch. You hold the keys.
  • Built for bad signal. Designed to connect and stay connected on wireless and cellular links that drop and recover.

What it is not

EigenTunnel does not modify, patch or replace crane software or any equipment vendor's systems, and it does not remove hardware. Crane-specific requirements come from the Coast Guard and your Captain of the Port. EigenTunnel works alongside your segmentation, monitoring and vendor-access controls. It does not replace them.

A small, low-risk next step

  1. A 20-minute look. One call about one link that gives you trouble. No deck, no data shared.
  2. A lab pilot or one scan. Test one connection with synthetic traffic, or scan one approved segment, with written acceptance criteria. Nothing touches operations without your team present.
