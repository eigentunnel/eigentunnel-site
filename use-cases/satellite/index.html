Use case · Satellite

Post-quantum security that stays up over satellite.

Satellite links drop packets, add delay and sit behind carrier NAT. EigenTunnel gives them quantum-resistant protection that connects and holds, so remote sites, vehicles and teams stay secure on the link they actually have.

What you get

Connects through satellite NAT

EigenTunnel reaches endpoints that sit behind carrier-grade NAT, the same setup our founder used in a relayed test with the far endpoint behind a Starlink connection.

Holds through fades and handovers

Built for links that drop and recover. Sessions stay protected through the rough patches instead of failing and starting over.

Light on expensive bandwidth

Designed to keep protection overhead low on links where every byte costs money.

Ready for quantum

Quantum-resistant key exchange protects traffic that adversaries could record today and try to decrypt later.

Where it fits

  • Remote sites and field offices on GEO or LEO service
  • Vehicles, vessels and aircraft with satellite backhaul
  • Expeditionary and disaster-response teams
  • Telemetry from pipelines, wells and substations

Why now

Published research shows post-quantum handshakes can fail on lossy satellite links because larger keys and certificates fragment. NDSS SpaceSec 2026. EigenTunnel is built for exactly that environment.

How to start

  1. Book a 20-minute call about your link.
  2. Agree on one connection and what success looks like.
  3. Run a time-boxed pilot on your own link and decide with the results in hand.
