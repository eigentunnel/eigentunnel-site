EigenScan · Free design-partner pilot

Encrypted data stolen today can be unlocked tomorrow.

U.S. security agencies warn that attackers may be stealing encrypted data today and storing it, so they can unlock it once quantum computers are powerful enough. Many companies also still use old, weak encryption in the systems that run their plants, rail lines, and networks, often without knowing where it is. Some intruders may already be inside: hidden malware, sometimes built into equipment, quietly 'phones home' to its operators for months without being noticed.

What EigenScan does

EigenScan is a small appliance that works entirely inside your network and finds weak and quantum-vulnerable encryption, designed to be gentle with operations and control equipment, and it sends nothing to any outside cloud.

Why it helps

See where you are exposed

A clear list of where weak or quantum-vulnerable encryption is running, so you fix what matters first.

Gentle on operations

It moves slowly, touches only what your team approves, and can be stopped at any moment.

Your data stays with you

Nothing leaves your network. You keep every result.

The free pilot

3 spots only
  • Our founder comes to your site in person.
  • We scan one network segment that you choose and approve in advance.
  • You keep the results, with a plain-English list of what to fix first.
  • Optional outbound watch: EigenScan can also listen for devices on your network that are quietly phoning home to someone outside, a common sign of hidden malware.
  • It takes about a day of your team’s time. No cost and no obligation.

What we have shown in our lab

  • In our lab, EigenScan separated the services still using only today’s quantum-vulnerable encryption from those already using the newer, quantum-resistant kind.
  • In our lab, it flagged older, weaker settings, such as an outdated version of encryption that should have been retired.
  • In our lab, it scanned at a slow, capped pace, left alone the machines it was told to skip, and slowed down further when machines went quiet.
