OMB M-26-15 in plain language

In June 2026 the White House turned federal post-quantum planning into deadlines. Executive Order 14412 set the direction, and two days later OMB Memorandum M-26-15 told civilian agencies how to execute it. Here is what the memo asks for, when, and what it means for the teams doing the work.

The short version

  • Who: federal civilian agencies. National Security Systems follow the NSA's separate CNSA 2.0 track. [3]
  • First deadline: a PQC migration plan to OMB and ONCD within 120 days of the memo, which works out to about October 22, 2026. [2]
  • End state: full migration of remaining federal civilian systems by 2035, in line with NSM-10. [2]

What the migration plan must contain

Reported summaries of the memo list these required elements: [2]

  1. Risk-based prioritization of systems
  2. Phased timelines
  3. An automated cryptographic inventory method
  4. A crypto-agility architecture plan
  5. Coordination with third parties and vendors
  6. Resource estimates
  7. Governance roles

Inventory comes first

The memo directs agencies toward automated inventories and a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM): a structured list of where each algorithm, key, certificate, protocol and library is in use. [2] The formal CBOM standard is not expected until about March 2027, so agencies are building toward it now rather than waiting.

Hybrid is a bridge, not the destination

Summaries note the memo describes hybrid classical-plus-post-quantum architectures as an intricate and resource-intensive stopgap. [2] In practice, plan for crypto-agility: systems that can change algorithms by configuration instead of by replacement.

What to do this quarter

  1. Find your cryptography. Start with network-facing services and the links you cannot easily patch.
  2. Rank systems by how long their data must stay secret and how hard they are to change.
  3. Pick one hard connection and run a scoped post-quantum pilot with written acceptance criteria.
