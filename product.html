Product

One overlay. Two ways to run it.

EigenTunnel Core does the cryptographic work for lab and evaluation: it cloaks enrolled nodes and protects data in flight with interop-grade hybrid post-quantum key exchange and signatures — free, self-managed, no account. EigenTunnel Cloud is where production runs, with a locked CNSA 2.0 profile plus everything a fleet needs that one node can't do for itself: who gets to join, what's actually protected, and how to reach devices that can't be reached directly.

Cryptography

Protect traffic with a long shelf life.

Harvest-now-decrypt-later is the primary threat model: an adversary records ciphertext today and decrypts it once a cryptographically-relevant quantum computer exists. Anything that needs to stay confidential for years — not days — is exposed under classical-only crypto right now.

Hybrid tunnels

An attacker has to break two independent algorithms, not one, to read your traffic. X25519 + ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) key establishment, AES-256-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305 for bulk encryption — classical and post-quantum combined, so the session stays safe even if one primitive is later broken.

Mutual authentication

Every peer proves who they are before a byte moves — there's no anonymous listener mode to misconfigure. Core's interop profile uses ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204) signatures.

CNSA 2.0 for production

Cloud runs a locked profile — ML-KEM-1024 + ML-DSA-87 (Category 5) + AES-256-GCM — enforced at process start and at peer admission, with no mid-session downgrade. Forward secrecy is per-session either way.

Connectivity

Connect when the network will not cooperate.

NAT, CGNAT, and locked-down networks stop most overlays cold. EigenTunnel doesn't need an inbound port on either end — two paths, same cryptography underneath.

When a direct path exists, nodes connect over the overlay directly. When NAT, CGNAT, or a restrictive network gets in the way, the gateway relay accepts leaf sessions only — it never dials your phone, your Starlink home connection, or a hotel laptop. Both endpoints reach out to the gateway; the gateway never reaches in.
