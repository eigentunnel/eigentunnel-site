Executive brief

Fund a clear decision before a wider deployment.

EigenTunnel is developing quantum-resistant encrypted connections for selected traffic between existing systems. Its relay/gateway (an intermediate connection point for systems that cannot connect directly) has been tested across a difficult network boundary. A complete self-service offering of EigenTunnel Console (our paid, managed service for setting up and running EigenTunnel connections) is still in development.

When a conversation makes sense

You operate long-lived systems

You need to evaluate connection-level protection while understanding endpoint, safety and integration constraints.

You build equipment or services

You want to investigate adding this capability to a product and need to establish engineering effort, licensing and support requirements.

Why pay for an evaluation?

The proposed value is a narrower, better-informed migration decision. A scoped engagement can define the connection to test, the agreed measurements, integration requirements and unresolved limitations. It should end with evidence you can use to proceed, revise the approach or stop.

Savings, deployment speed and operational impact must be measured in your environment. They are not established product guarantees.

What to agree before signing

  1. The business problem and one named connection.
  2. Approved systems, test environment, exclusions and customer responsibilities.
  3. Deliverables, measurements and acceptance criteria.
  4. Price, schedule, support and data-handling terms.
  5. The decision owner and the criteria for further investment.

What is ready—and what is pending

Founder-tested relay connectivity: macOS® → AWS (Amazon Web Services) → macOS, including a remote endpoint behind Starlink satellite internet and its NAT (network address translation, which makes direct connections difficult). Separately, the private CNSA 2.0 algorithm profile (CNSA is the NSA’s Commercial National Security Algorithm Suite) has developer-test evidence. The Console’s payment and provisioning remain unfinished; fleet workflows (managing many devices and sites together) remain roadmap. Independent assessment and production evidence are pending.

A pilot is not a purchase of an independently validated, generally available managed service. Formal authorization is not conferred by the available evidence drafts.

Measured on November 10, 2026

A live Cat-M road test, checked at the receiver.

A rail-style rig (an STM32-class board, a Cat-M modem, and a Dell relay) ran a live road test over a commercial LTE Cat-M link. The route went from Omaha to Ashland and Greenwood, Nebraska, and back: 66.5 miles, alongside BNSF along US-6.

19 of 19

Drive transfers delivered and verified at the AWS (Amazon Web Services) receiver, plus 20 more after lunch.

3 for 3

Unplug/replug passes self-recovered to verified delivery with no human help.

122 bytes

Session resume, measured. A full handshake is about 8 KB.

Session security was a fresh authenticated post-quantum handshake: ML-KEM-1024 key exchange and ML-DSA-87 signatures, with no pre-shared keys, so there is no central key database to steal. The tests ran on Red Hat® Enterprise Linux® 9.8 with the operating system in FIPS mode.

These figures are the measured results of that test. They do not establish a rail deployment, a safety approval, or a service-level commitment.
