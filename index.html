Why now

Attacks don't wait for a human anymore.

The newest frontier AI models can already find unknown vulnerabilities and chain them into working exploits against hardened systems end to end, without a person guiding each step. That capability is gated behind vetted access today. Gates don't hold forever — they get cloned, leaked, and matched by labs with fewer scruples. By the time it's common tooling, the network you're protecting needs to already be the kind of target that has nothing to scan.

Nothing to enumerate

No open inbound port on either end. An automated scanner finds no listener to fingerprint in the first place.

No anonymous handshake

Every session has to prove a registered identity before it opens. There's no unauthenticated foothold to probe.

Why it matters

The traffic you encrypt today can be read tomorrow.

An attacker doesn't need to break your encryption now — they just need to record the ciphertext and wait for a quantum computer that can. That's "harvest now, decrypt later," and it's already happening. If what you're protecting needs to stay confidential for years — credentials, telemetry, IP, anything long-lived — it's exposed under classical-only crypto right now, not someday.

Hybrid tunnels

Break one algorithm and the connection still holds. X25519 + ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) key exchange, AES-256-GCM or ChaCha20-Poly1305 bulk encryption.

Mutual auth

No anonymous listeners, ever. Core's interop profile uses ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204); Cloud's locked CNSA 2.0 profile steps up to ML-DSA-87.

CNSA 2.0, assessment pending

Cloud runs a fail-closed profile — ML-KEM-1024 + ML-DSA-87 (Category 5) + AES-256-GCM — enforced at startup and at peer admission. Implemented with developer-test evidence; production evidence and independent assessment are pending.
